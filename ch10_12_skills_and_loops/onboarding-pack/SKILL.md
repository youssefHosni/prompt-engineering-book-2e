  a starter pack or build a first-week plan. Do not use to grant access,

A first week someone can actually follow, assembled from what exists.

## Steps

1. Confirm the role, the team and the start date.
2. Confirm whether the person is new to the company or moving internally.
3. An internal move needs a different pack; most of day one is already done.
4. Read `references/access-matrix.md` for the role.
5. List every system the role requires, with the approver for each.
6. Mark which approvals have a lead time longer than one day.
7. Those must be requested before the start date, not on it.
8. Draft the access request list with approvers and lead times.
9. Never request access. Produce the list for a human to submit.
10. Read the team's runbooks directory and list every runbook.
11. Select the three a newcomer will need in week one.
12. Selecting more than three guarantees none is read.
13. Identify the team's on-call rotation and when the person joins it.
14. A start date inside an on-call week is a scheduling problem; flag it.
15. Identify the person's first task from the tracker.
16. A good first task is small, reviewed, and ships. Say why the chosen one qualifies.
17. Identify who will review that first task.
18. Identify the buddy and confirm they are not on leave in week one.
19. Draft day one: accounts, laptop, the handbook sections that apply to the role.
20. Keep day one to four items. The rest will not land.
21. Draft day two: environment setup, with the runbook that covers it.
22. Draft day three: read the three selected runbooks, meet the buddy.
23. Draft day four: first task, paired.
24. Draft day five: first task continued, review, retrospective with the buddy.
25. For each day, state what done looks like.
26. A day with no completion criterion is a day that slips.
27. List the recurring meetings the role attends, with their purpose.
28. Omit any meeting the person does not need in month one.
29. List the three people the person should meet in week two, and why.
30. Check every document linked in the pack exists and is current.
31. A dead link in an onboarding pack is the first thing the person finds.
32. Check the handbook sections cited are the current revision.
33. Flag any runbook last updated more than six months ago.
34. Assemble the pack in the order the person will meet it.
35. Put the access list first; it is the part with lead times.
36. STOP. Show the pack to the hiring manager before sending.
37. Incorporate their corrections to the first task and the buddy.
38. On approval, write to `onboarding/<name>-<start-date>.md`.
39. Record in `PROGRESS.md` which runbooks were stale at step 33.
40. Never send the pack directly to the new starter.

## Rules

- Never request or grant access. Produce the list; a human submits it.
- Never link a document without confirming it exists and is current.
- Never put more than four items in day one.
- Never select more than three runbooks for week one.
- Flag a start date that lands inside the team's on-call week.
- The pack goes to the hiring manager, never straight to the starter.
